# Chapter 1: At the Table — Dining Manners

**Virtue: Respect | সম্মান [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION]**

**Learning Objective:** Students will demonstrate 5 correct dining manners consistently.

---

### Our Story: Dinner at Nanu's House

It was Eid dinner at Nanu's house. The smell of rice, dal, and chicken curry filled the whole room. Tariq was so hungry that the moment the food was placed on the table, he grabbed the biggest piece of chicken — even though Nanu had not yet sat down.

He used his left hand to scoop rice, spoke loudly with his mouth full, and within ten minutes, had left half his plate uneaten. "I'm full!" he said, pushing back his chair.

Nanu sat down quietly. She looked at Tariq with kind eyes. "Come, let me show you something," she said.

She explained: we wait until our elders are seated before we begin. We eat with our right hand — this is the way of our faith and our culture. We chew slowly, with our mouths closed, so that others can enjoy their meal peacefully. We say **Bismillah** (বিসমিল্লাহ [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION]) before we start and **Alhamdulillah** (আলহামদুলিল্লাহ [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION]) when we finish. And we try to finish what is on our plate — because food is a gift, and wasting it is disrespectful to the one who cooked it and to those who have less.

Tariq listened. The next day at breakfast, he tried all five things. Nanu noticed, and she smiled the biggest smile he had ever seen.

*[Illustration: A warm family dinner scene. Tariq sits politely, right hand on food, waiting as Nanu takes her seat. Speech bubble shows "Bismillah!"]*

---

### Key Dining Manners

**1. Wait for elders and adults before starting to eat.**
When someone older than you is still sitting down or has not yet been served, wait. This shows respect for them.

**2. Eat with the right hand.**
In Bangladeshi culture and Islamic practice, eating with the right hand is a sign of cleanliness and respect. [SOURCE_ALT_07] We use the right hand for eating and for giving and receiving things.

**3. Do not talk with food in your mouth.**
Finish chewing and swallowing before you speak. Talking with a full mouth is uncomfortable for others to see and hear.

**4. Chew quietly — do not slurp.**
Eat at a calm pace. Loud slurping or smacking sounds disturb others at the table.

**5. Do not waste food — finish what is on your plate.**
Take only what you can eat. If you are unsure how much you want, take a little and ask for more later. Wasting food is disrespectful to those who worked hard to prepare it and to those who do not have enough.

**6. Say Bismillah before eating and Alhamdulillah after.**
Bismillah (بِسْمِ اللَّهِ) means "In the name of Allah." Alhamdulillah (الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ) means "All praise is for Allah." These words remind us to be grateful for our food. [SOURCE_ALT_07][SOURCE_ALT_08]

# Chapter 2: Clean Hands, Healthy Me — Extended Hygiene

**Virtue: Discipline | শৃঙ্খলা [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION]**

**Learning Objective:** Students will explain when and how to wash hands properly.

---

### Our Story: Rafi's Tummy Ache

Rafi had been playing outside all afternoon. His hands were dusty and his fingers had soil under the nails. When his mother called him for lunch, he ran straight to the table and started eating. He did the same thing the next day. And the day after that.

On the third day, Rafi's tummy started to hurt. By evening, he had a fever. His mother took him to the doctor.

Dr. Nasrin examined Rafi carefully. "Tell me," she said, "do you wash your hands before eating?"

Rafi looked at the floor. "Not always."

Dr. Nasrin smiled kindly. "Tiny things called germs live on our hands. We cannot see them, but they travel from our hands into our food, and then into our body. That is how many children get sick."

She taught Rafi the six steps of proper handwashing. "Twenty seconds of scrubbing — that is all it takes to wash the germs away," she said.

Rafi went home determined. He stuck the six steps on the bathroom wall. He washed his hands before every meal, after every visit to the toilet, and after playing outside. Within three days, he was completely well again. And he has not forgotten since.

*[Illustration: Six-panel diagram showing each handwashing step with friendly cartoon hands. Final panel: Rafi eating happily, hands clean, smile on face.]*

---

### The Six Steps of Handwashing

**Step 1 — Wet**
Turn on the tap. Wet both hands completely with clean water.

**Step 2 — Soap**
Apply soap to your palms. Any soap is fine — bar soap or liquid soap both work.

**Step 3 — Lather (20 seconds)**
Rub your palms together to make lather. Count slowly to twenty — or sing the alphabet song once all the way through. This is how long the soap needs to work.

**Step 4 — Scrub between fingers and under nails**
Interlace your fingers and rub them together. Use your fingernails of one hand to scrub the palm of the other. Clean under each nail.

**Step 5 — Rinse**
Hold your hands under running water and rinse off all the soap. Make sure no soap is left on your skin.

**Step 6 — Dry**
Dry your hands with a clean towel or shake them dry in the air. Wet hands pick up more germs than dry hands.

---

### When Must You Wash Your Hands?

- Before eating any food or drink
- After using the toilet
- After playing outside or touching animals
- After sneezing or coughing into your hands
- After touching rubbish or dirty objects

---

### More Clean Habits

**Brushing your teeth:** Brush your teeth twice every day — once in the morning after waking up, and once at night before sleeping. Use a small amount of toothpaste and brush for two minutes. Healthy teeth help you eat well and speak clearly.

**Bathing regularly:** Bathe every day, or at least every other day, especially in hot weather. Soap away sweat and dirt from your body. Pay attention to behind your ears, between your toes, and under your arms.

**Clean nails:** Keep your nails short and clean. Long nails trap dirt and germs underneath. Ask a parent or adult to help you trim your nails once a week.

# Chapter 3: My Space, Your Space — Respecting Boundaries

**Virtue: Respect | সম্মান [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION]**

**Learning Objective:** Students will ask before touching others' belongings and knock before entering.

---

### Our Story: Mitu and the Pen

Mitu's older sister Priya kept her study room very tidy. Her books were arranged in a row, her pens in a cup, and her notebooks in a neat pile.

One afternoon, Mitu needed a pen. Instead of asking, she simply walked into Priya's room, picked up the blue pen from the cup, used it, and left it on the dining table.

When Priya came home and sat down to study, her pen was gone. She searched everywhere. When she finally found it on the table, she was upset. "Mitu, you took my pen without asking and didn't put it back. That is my space and my things."

Mitu felt sorry. She had not meant to cause a problem. She had just wanted a pen.

Their mother sat down with them both. "Every person — even inside a family — has their own space and their own things," she explained. "To respect someone means to treat their space the way you would want your space to be treated."

The next day, Mitu knocked on Priya's door. "Apa, may I please borrow your blue pen? I will give it back as soon as I'm done." Priya smiled and handed it over. The room felt warmer than before.

*[Illustration: Two panels side by side. Left panel: Mitu sneaking into room, looking guilty. Right panel: Mitu knocking on the door, smiling, Priya opening the door and handing her the pen warmly.]*

---

### Rules for Respecting Space and Belongings

**1. Knock before entering.**
Before you enter anyone's room — a family member's, a teacher's, anyone's — knock on the door and wait. If the door is open, say "Excuse me" and wait for them to acknowledge you before entering.

**2. Ask before borrowing.**
Even if it is something small — a pencil, an eraser, a book — always ask first. Say: "May I borrow your ___?" or "Can I please use your ___ for a moment?"

**3. Return things promptly.**
When you borrow something, give it back as soon as you are done with it. Do not leave it somewhere else. Return it to the exact place you took it from.

**4. Do not read others' notebooks or letters.**
Someone else's notebook, diary, or letter is private. Reading it without permission — even out of curiosity — is a form of disrespect.

**5. Do not touch classmates' bags or belongings without permission.**
At school, everyone has their own bag and desk space. Do not rummage through a friend's bag or take things from their desk — even if you think they won't mind. Always ask.

# Chapter 4: Kindness Everywhere — Acts of Kindness at Home

**Virtue: Kindness | দয়া [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION]**

### Our Story: Rafi's Gift to Amma

Rafi came home from school on a Tuesday and found his mother sitting on the sofa with her eyes half closed. She had been to the market in the morning, cooked lunch, washed clothes, and cleaned the house — all before noon.

Usually Rafi would drop his bag on the floor, ask for a snack, and turn on the TV. But today he stopped and really looked at his mother. He could see she was tired.

He put his bag away quietly. He went to the kitchen, poured a glass of cool water, and brought it to her with both hands. "Amma, your water," he said softly.

She opened her eyes and smiled.

Then Rafi took his own plate to the kitchen after his snack — without being asked. And when his little sister Tania started to fuss, he sat beside her and showed her how to stack blocks, so his mother could rest for a little longer.

That evening, his mother hugged him. "Rafi," she said, "you did not give me any big thing today. But you made my whole day better. That is what kindness is."

Rafi felt something warm in his chest. He decided he would try to notice when people needed help — and act, before they had to ask.

*[Illustration: Rafi offering water to his mother with a gentle smile. Mother reaches out to receive it. Small inset panels: Rafi taking his plate to the kitchen, Rafi playing with Tania.]*

### What is Kindness?

Kindness is choosing to do something good for another person — not because you have to, but because you want to make their life a little easier or happier.

Kindness does not have to be big. The smallest actions — a smile, a glass of water, picking something up for someone — can change a person's entire day.

### Small Acts of Kindness You Can Do at Home

- Bring a glass of water to a tired family member.
- Put your plate and cup in the kitchen after eating.
- Play quietly when a sibling or parent is resting.
- Help set the table before meals.
- Help clear the table after meals.
- Fold your own clothes and put them away.
- Say "good morning" and "good night" to everyone in the family.
- Hug a family member who seems sad.
- Offer to help a younger sibling with something they find hard.
- Say "thank you" when someone does something for you.

# Chapter 5: Home Rules, Happy Home

**Virtue: Discipline | শৃঙ্খলা [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION]**

**Learning Objective:** Students will name 4 home etiquette rules and explain why each matters.

---

### Our Story: Two Homes

*Imagine two homes, side by side.*

In the first home, the children shout across the house when they want something. The lights in every room are always on, even empty ones. Bags and shoes are dropped wherever someone stops walking. When dinner is on the table, the children sit down without helping and leave without clearing. When a parent asks for help, the answer is usually "in a minute" — and the minute never comes.

In the second home, the children speak at a normal volume indoors. When they leave a room, they check the light switch. Shoes go by the door, bags go on the hook. Before dinner, one child helps set the table; after dinner, another helps carry plates to the kitchen. When a parent asks for help, the answer is "okay" — and they actually go.

*Both homes have the same number of people. The same size. The same food. But which one feels calmer? Which one feels happier? Why?*

The difference is not luck. It is habits — small, daily rules that everyone in the home agrees to follow. These rules do not make life harder. They make life smoother. And when everyone follows them together, the home becomes a place where everyone — children and adults — feels at ease.

*[Illustration: Side-by-side panels. Left: chaotic home — clutter, lights blazing, children shouting, parent looking stressed. Right: calm home — tidy, one light on, children helping, parent smiling.]*

---

### Home Etiquette Rules

**Rule 1: No shouting indoors.**
If you need someone, walk to them or speak at a calm, indoor volume. Shouting across rooms is startling and disrespectful. Even if you are excited, use your "inside voice."

**Rule 2: Tidy your own belongings.**
Put your bag, shoes, and clothes where they belong when you come home. Do not leave your things for others to move. Your belongings are your responsibility.

**Rule 3: Turn off lights when leaving a room.**
Before you leave any room, check the light. If you were the last one in it, turn it off. This saves electricity, saves money for your family, and is good for the environment.

**Rule 4: Help set and clear the table.**
Mealtimes are shared moments. Helping to prepare for them and clean up afterwards is a way of contributing to the family — even if it is one small task.

**Rule 5: Speak respectfully to everyone in the home.**
This includes parents, grandparents, older siblings, younger siblings, and any household helpers. Use polite words. Use a calm tone. No snapping, no mocking, no ignoring.

*[Teacher note: Write the shared rules on the board as students offer them. After the activity, you will likely have 15–20 rules collected from the whole class. Point out how many rules overlap — this helps students see that these are community norms, not just individual preferences. [SOURCE_BD_11]]*
