## 2. Research Foundation

### Evidence Base

This campaign is grounded in four evidence sources that collectively justify both the problem and the social-media-first strategy.

---

**[SOURCE_BD_13]** — *Social media usage patterns of Bangladeshi secondary school students*

Bangladeshi secondary school students are heavy social media consumers, with Facebook and YouTube dominating daily usage. Content exposure during formative years directly influences attitudes, vocabulary, and behavioural expectations. The Bhadra Bangladesh campaign treats this existing high-use environment as an asset rather than an obstacle: if students are already online, the campaign brings positive behavioural content into the feed rather than asking students to step away from screens.

---

**[SOURCE_BD_14]** — *Misuse of social media by teenagers in Bangladesh*

Research documenting social media misuse among Bangladeshi teenagers identifies a pattern in which exposure to aggressive, disrespectful, or uncivil online content is associated with declining interpersonal manners in offline settings. This source provides the problem diagnosis: the absence of positive behavioural modelling online creates a vacuum that is filled by content that normalises incivility. The Bhadra Bangladesh campaign exists to fill that vacuum with alternatives.

---

**[SOURCE_BD_16]** — *Gen Z attitudes shaped by social media norms*

This is the strategic cornerstone of the campaign. Social media algorithms serve users more of what they engage with. If polite behaviour, respectful language, and etiquette-forward content accumulates engagement signals (likes, shares, comments, saves), the algorithm will distribute it further — making good manners progressively more visible as a norm. The insight from [SOURCE_BD_16] is used offensively: the same mechanism that has spread negative behavioural norms can be captured to spread positive ones. Every share of a Bhadra Bangladesh post is a micro-intervention in algorithmic norm-setting.

---

**[SOURCE_ALT_05]** — *UNICEF Social Behaviour Change (SBC) Guidance*

UNICEF's Social Behaviour Change framework confirms that behaviour change communication campaigns work most effectively when they (a) reach audiences through platforms they already trust and use, (b) use peer modelling and aspirational social proof, and (c) engage community gatekeepers (parents, teachers, religious leaders) as amplifiers rather than targets alone. The Bhadra Bangladesh campaign applies all three principles: using existing platforms, modelling polite behaviour through relatable characters and real children, and activating parents and teachers as community champions.

---

### Strategic Rationale

> If social media shapes behavioural norms negatively, it can also be used to shape them positively.

The Bhadra Bangladesh campaign does not ask families to reduce screen time. It asks Blu Foundation to become the most compelling etiquette-positive voice in the Bangladeshi social media feed — creating a positive feedback loop where engagement with good-manners content trains algorithms to show more of it.

## 3. Platform Strategy

| Platform | Primary Audience | Content Type | Posting Frequency | Language |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Facebook** | Parents, teachers, general public | Photo posts, short videos (1–3 min), carousels, Facebook Lives, community group posts | 5 posts/week (main page) + daily group activity | Bengali (primary), English (secondary for reach) |
| **YouTube** | Teachers, parents, general public | Long-form campaign videos (3–5 min), classroom etiquette tutorials, expert interviews, YouTube Shorts | 1 long-form video/week + 2–3 Shorts/week | Bengali with English subtitles |
| **TikTok** | Youth age 12+, general public | 15–30 second challenge videos, trending audio with etiquette visuals, duet-friendly formats | 7 posts/week (daily) | Bengali (conversational), some English hooks |
| **Instagram** | Secondary: urban parents, youth, design-led audience | Reels, static quote cards, Stories, Highlights | 4 posts/week + daily Stories | Bengali and English bilingual captions |
| **LinkedIn** | Donors, CSR partners, education NGOs, school administrators | Programme updates, impact reports, partnership announcements, thought leadership | 2 posts/week | English (primary) |

### Platform-Specific Notes

**Facebook** is the highest-priority platform for Blu Foundation's primary audiences. Facebook's ad targeting tools allow precise demographic segmentation by district, age, parental status, and interest category — enabling cost-effective reach to Bangladeshi parents. The Facebook Group (see Section 9) acts as the community anchor.

**TikTok** is the primary platform for youth audience penetration (aligned with [SOURCE_BD_16] findings on Gen Z norm-shaping). Short, challenge-based formats with trending sounds maximise organic algorithmic reach. TikTok content should be distinctly "younger" in tone than Facebook — energetic, fast-paced, humorous.

**YouTube** serves a dual purpose: a library of durable campaign content (tutorials, stories, expert panels) that continues to generate views long after posting, and a Shorts channel to capture younger viewers already using YouTube as an entertainment platform.

**LinkedIn** is not a mass-reach platform in Bangladesh but serves a critical function: it is where corporate CSR decision-makers and international NGO partners discover and evaluate organisations. All LinkedIn content should position Blu Foundation as a credible, impact-driven institution.

## 8. Influencer Engagement Framework

### Tier 1 — Micro-Influencers (10,000–100,000 followers)

**Profile:** Parenting bloggers, education content creators, school-based content accounts, family lifestyle creators based in Bangladesh (Dhaka, Chittagong, Sylhet, Rajshahi, Khulna preferred for geographic spread).

**Why this tier:** Per [SOURCE_ALT_05] SBC guidance, micro-influencers have higher audience trust and engagement rates than celebrities. For behaviour change communication — where credibility matters more than reach — a parenting micro-influencer saying "my child learned to say please because of Blu Foundation" is more persuasive than a celebrity endorsement.

**Engagement approach:**
- Identify: Search Facebook/Instagram/TikTok for hashtags #BangladeshParenting #DhakaParents #BangladeshMom #BangladeshDad — manually review top creators
- Approach: Warm DM or email, introduce Blu Foundation's mission, offer collaboration
- Brief: Provide full content brief (see below) + 2-week turnaround
- Compensation: Content gifting (Blu Foundation educational materials, branded kit) + social recognition; no cash payment at Tier 1 unless budget allows

**Minimum 5 Tier 1 partnerships targeted for campaign launch.**

---

### Tier 2 — Mid-Tier Influencers (100,000–500,000 followers)

**Profile:** Lifestyle and family content creators with established Bangladesh audiences. May cover cooking, home, family travel, fashion — but must have family/child-positive content history.

**Why this tier:** Broader reach to parents who do not follow education-specific accounts. Mid-tier influencers on TikTok and Facebook can generate significant organic amplification at comparatively low cost versus celebrity.

**Engagement approach:**
- Identify: Review Facebook Pages and TikTok accounts with 100K–500K Bangladeshi followers; cross-check engagement rate (target >3% engagement — bot-heavy accounts will have high followers, low engagement)
- Approach: Formal email outreach to business email listed in profile bio
- Brief: Full content brief + 3-week turnaround + one revision round
- Compensation: Paid partnership — rate to be negotiated [FOUNDER INPUT REQUIRED: influencer budget and any existing personal network connections]

**Target 2–3 Tier 2 partnerships for Weeks 3–10 of campaign.**

### Tier 3 — Celebrity Ambassador (if budget allows)

**Profile:** A Bangladeshi cultural figure with established family-values public image. Suitable categories include: nationally respected actor or actress who is also a public parent, cricketer with family-forward public persona, television presenter with education/children's content background.

**Why this tier:** A single celebrity endorsement can generate earned media (news coverage, organic shares) that delivers reach no paid advertising budget can replicate. Even one post from a respected public figure can reframe Bhadra Bangladesh from "NGO campaign" to "national movement."

**Considerations:**
- Celebrity involvement requires highest cultural sensitivity review — any controversy around the ambassador reflects on Blu Foundation
- Fee negotiation may require formal MOU and invoicing
- Recommend a one-time "Brand Ambassador Launch" post rather than ongoing contract for budget efficiency

## 9. Community Building Strategy

### Facebook Group: "Bhadra Parents — ভদ্র অভিভাবক"

**Group type:** Closed (members must request to join — reviewed by admin)
**Language:** Bengali (primary), English allowed
**Description:** A community for parents across Bangladesh who believe in raising polite, respectful, and confident children. Moderated by Blu Foundation. [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION for group description]

**Why a closed group:** A closed group creates a sense of belonging and safety — parents are more likely to share personal stories and ask genuine questions in a closed community than on a public page. The group also provides Blu Foundation with a direct, algorithm-independent channel to its most engaged audience.

**Launch strategy:**
- Seed the group in Week 1 with 50–100 founding members (Blu Foundation team, partner school contacts, early followers)
- Post daily in the group during Weeks 1–4 to establish the habit of engagement
- Invite all users who comment on campaign posts to join the group
- Pin a welcome post from the founder

**Ongoing group content:**

| Content Type | Frequency | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Tip of the Week | Weekly (Monday) | Single actionable manners tip for parents to try at home |
| Question prompt | Weekly (Wednesday) | "How did your child surprise you with good manners this week?" |
| Expert resource share | Bi-weekly | Article, video, or infographic on child behaviour and etiquette |
| Milestone celebration | Monthly | Campaign reach update, challenge winner announcement |
| Facebook Live simulcast | Monthly | Live sessions (see below) broadcast into the group first |

---

### Monthly Live Sessions with Experts

**Format:** Facebook Live, 45–60 minutes, first broadcast into the group then saved to the page
**Cadence:** Once per month, starting Month 2 (Week 5 or 6)
**Host:** Blu Foundation representative, with guest expert

**Recommended guest expert categories:**
- Child psychologist with Dhaka practice — discussing the psychological basis of manners and social behaviour
- Primary school head teacher — sharing classroom etiquette strategies
- Child development specialist or UNICEF/Save the Children Bangladesh representative
- Islamic scholar (for sessions touching on akhlaq / Islamic etiquette values, reaching religiously observant families) — requires Islamic scholar advisory review (see Section 12)

**Promotion:** Promote live sessions 5 days in advance across all platforms. Save recordings to YouTube after broadcast.

---

### User-Generated Content: "Share Your Child's Polite Moment"

**Initiative name:** #BhadraKid
**Launch:** Week 3 (ongoing)
**Platform:** Facebook (primary), Instagram (secondary)

**Mechanic:**
1. Blu Foundation posts a prompt post inviting parents to share a photo or short video of their child demonstrating a polite behaviour, with the hashtag #BhadraKid and a caption describing what the child did.
2. Blu Foundation admin reviews all tagged posts within 24 hours.
3. Selected submissions are reshared to the Blu Foundation page and group (with explicit permission requested via comment or DM).
4. Weekly "Bhadra Kid of the Week" highlight — reshared with a congratulations message and a digital recognition certificate.

**Moderation rules:** All UGC featuring children must include explicit parental permission (obtained via comment on the post, e.g., "Blu Foundation, you have our permission to share this"). A UGC moderation log should be maintained by the content team.

### Bangladesh Cultural Sensitivity Review Gate

Every piece of content must be reviewed against the following checklist before approval:

- [ ] Does the content represent the diversity of Bangladesh? (district, urban/rural, economic background)
- [ ] Are all children depicted with appropriate clothing and appearance for a family-values audience?
- [ ] Does any content inadvertently exclude or stereotype by religion (Muslim majority, Hindu minority, Buddhist minority communities in Bangladesh)?
- [ ] Is the Bengali language register appropriate? (Avoid overly formal/archaic Bengali that feels distant from everyday urban parents; avoid slang that may be inappropriate for a children's education brand)
- [ ] Does any content reference gender roles in ways that may be controversial? (Review against the brand's own values position)
- [ ] Are all child participants covered by signed parental/guardian consent forms?
- [ ] Has the content been checked for any unintentional political connotations? (Bangladesh's political environment requires constant awareness)

**Who conducts this review:** The Bengali Copywriter and one member of the Blu Foundation leadership team (ideally the founder or a Board member with Bangladesh cultural expertise).

---

### Islamic Scholar Advisory Review

For any content that:
- References Islamic values, akhlaq (moral character), adab (Islamic etiquette), or Quranic/Hadith sources
- Features Islamic dress, prayer, or religious settings
- Is intended for Eid or Ramadan campaigns

The content must additionally be reviewed by an Islamic scholar or Islamic education advisor. This protects Blu Foundation from unintentional misrepresentation of Islamic teachings and builds trust with religiously observant families, who represent the majority of Blu Foundation's target audience.
