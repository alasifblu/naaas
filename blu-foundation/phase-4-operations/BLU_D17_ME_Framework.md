## 1. Overview and Purpose

### 1.1 Why M&E Matters for Blu Foundation

Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) is the systematic process by which Blu Foundation tracks whether its program is reaching children, whether those children are changing their behavior, and whether that change is durable enough to matter in their lives and communities.

For Blu Foundation, M&E serves three equally important functions:

**Donor Accountability.** Donors — whether individual supporters, institutional funders, or government partners — are entitled to evidence that their investment is being used effectively. This framework provides the data infrastructure to produce honest, credible reports. It does not cherry-pick successes; it tracks what is not working as rigorously as what is working.

**Program Improvement.** M&E is not a reporting exercise conducted at year-end. It is a continuous feedback loop. Monthly coordinator reports, termly monitoring visits, and mid-year reviews exist so that the program team can identify problems — a curriculum unit that is not landing, a school where teacher confidence is low, a class level where behavioral outcomes are lagging — and make adjustments before the end of an academic year. M&E data is the primary input to Blu Foundation's annual program adaptation process.

**Evidence Building.** Bangladesh lacks a robust evidence base on school-based manners, etiquette, and character education. Blu Foundation occupies an important position: a program grounded in local culture and values, operating at the primary school level, with a structured curriculum and trained teachers. If the program generates credible, independently verified behavioral outcome data, it contributes to the national conversation about what holistic child development can look like. By Year 3, Blu Foundation should be in a position to publish findings and influence policy.

### 1.2 M&E Philosophy

Blu Foundation's M&E approach is guided by four principles:

**Participatory.** Children, teachers, parents, and school coordinators are not simply data sources. They are stakeholders whose observations, experiences, and feedback shape how the program is understood and improved. Focus groups, coordinator reports, and parent surveys are designed to surface perspectives from all four groups, not just the most easily measurable outputs.

**Strengths-Based.** Assessment tools are designed to capture growth, not to expose deficits. When a child moves from "Never" to "Sometimes" on a behavioral indicator, that is progress worth recording and celebrating. The framework avoids language that frames children as behaviorally deficient; it frames them as learners on a journey.

**Non-Punitive.** No data collected under this framework is used to penalize a school, a teacher, or a student. Poor outcomes on a monitoring visit trigger a support response from Blu Foundation, not a sanction. School coordinators are encouraged to report problems accurately because they know the response will be support, not blame. This is explicit in the School Partnership Agreement (BLU_D09).

**Age-Appropriate.** All student-facing instruments — the BAT-Student self-report, focus group facilitation guides, consent language — are designed for children aged 6–11. Language is simple, scenarios are relatable, and no child is asked to recall or report anything that could cause distress. Facilitation of student focus groups is restricted to trained Blu Foundation staff.

### 1.3 A Core Principle: Behavioral Change, Not Academic Marks

This framework measures behavioral change. It does not measure academic performance, and behavioral outcomes are not incorporated into academic grading or school reports.

This distinction is fundamental. Character education researchers have consistently found that integrating behavioral assessment into academic assessment systems undermines the authenticity of both [SOURCE_UK_02]. When children believe their manners are being graded in the same way as their mathematics, they perform etiquette rather than internalise it. Blu Foundation's BAT instruments are therefore explicitly non-academic: they do not appear on school report cards, they are not shared with class teachers as a component of academic assessment, and results are reported at aggregate (class-level or school-level) rather than individual level.

## 2. Theory of Change Summary

*(Full Theory of Change: see BLU_D03)*

Blu Foundation's Theory of Change holds that when primary school children (Class 1–5) in Bangladesh receive structured, culturally grounded instruction in manners and etiquette — delivered through a trained teacher, a purpose-designed textbook, and reinforced by parents and school environment — they develop observable, durable behavioral habits that improve their social relationships, contribute to a more respectful school culture, and equip them with interpersonal competencies that benefit them across their lives. The causal chain is: **quality curriculum + trained teachers + school environment → increased lesson exposure → student behavioral practice → observable behavioral change → improved peer relationships and school climate → long-term character formation**. Critical assumptions include that teachers deliver lessons with fidelity, that parents reinforce learning at home, and that school leadership creates an environment where respectful behavior is modeled and valued. The key risk is that structural pressures (exam focus, large class sizes, resource constraints) crowd out lesson time and reduce teacher engagement.

## 4. Blu Foundation Behavioral Assessment Tool (BAT)

### About the BAT

The Behavioral Assessment Tool is Blu Foundation's core student assessment instrument. It exists in three versions for three audiences: students (self-report), teachers (observation rubric), and parents (home survey). All three measure the same underlying construct — whether students are demonstrating the manners, etiquette, and interpersonal behaviors taught in the Blu Foundation curriculum — from different vantage points.

**What the BAT measures:** Observable behavior. Not knowledge of rules. Not ability to recite definitions. Behavioral assessments ask: *Is the child doing this?* not *Does the child know this?* This is consistent with the principle that character is assessed separately from academic knowledge [SOURCE_UK_02].

**Administration schedule:**
- **Baseline:** Start of program (first two weeks of Term 1, Year 1; at program start for newly joining schools thereafter)
- **Mid-point:** End of Term 1
- **Endline:** End of academic year (Term 3)

**Scoring:** Each item is scored 0 (Never), 1 (Sometimes), 2 (Often), 3 (Always). Maximum BAT-Student score: 30 (for 10 items). Results are reported at class level and school level. Individual scores are confidential.

**Language:** BAT-Student (Class 3–5) is administered in Bengali. BAT-Teacher and BAT-Parent are in Bengali. **[NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION — all three instruments to be professionally translated and back-translated before program launch]**

## 7. Monitoring Visit Protocol

### 7.1 Purpose

Monitoring visits are Blu Foundation's primary mechanism for direct observation of program quality. They serve three functions: to verify what coordinator reports are indicating, to identify quality issues not captured in written reports, and to provide direct support and encouragement to teachers and coordinators. Visits are scheduled (not surprise), and schools are informed at least one week in advance.

**Frequency:** Once per term per partner school (3 visits per academic year per school).

**Who conducts visits:** Program Manager (Year 1). M&E Officer when hired (Year 1 onward, once in post).

---

### 7.2 Pre-Visit Preparation (At Least 3 Days Before Visit)

- [ ] Review the last three monthly coordinator reports for the school. Note: lesson completion rates, attendance trends, any reported challenges, any behavioral observations worth following up.
- [ ] Review the most recent BAT-Teacher scores for the school (if available).
- [ ] Prepare the classroom observation schedule (which classes, which teachers).
- [ ] Confirm visit date and time with school coordinator.
- [ ] Confirm student focus group logistics: 6–8 students from Class 3–5, parental consent required, private space available, no teacher present during focus group.
- [ ] Review the school's BAT-Student baseline data to have comparison points during the visit.
- [ ] Bring: observation checklist (printed), monitoring visit report template, consent forms (if not pre-distributed), camera (only if school has signed media consent).

---

### 7.3 During the Visit

**Classroom Observation (30 minutes per class visited, minimum 2 classes)**

Observe a Blu Foundation lesson or a regular class period. Complete the classroom observation checklist (Section 7.5) during the observation. Do not intervene in the lesson. Note specific examples, not just impressions.

**Teacher Interview (20 minutes, semi-structured)**

Conduct after the classroom observation. Key questions:
1. How confident do you feel delivering the Blu Foundation curriculum? What parts feel most natural? What parts feel difficult?
2. Have you noticed any behavioral changes in your students since starting the program? Can you give me a specific example?
3. Are there any lessons or activities that students respond to particularly well? Any they disengage from?
4. Have parents mentioned anything to you about their child's behavior at home?
5. Is there anything Blu Foundation could do to better support you?
6. Do you have enough materials? Is the textbook meeting your needs?

**Student Focus Group (20 minutes, 6–8 students from Class 3–5)**

Facilitated by the visiting Blu Foundation staff. No teachers or school management present. Conducted in Bengali. Age-appropriate, informal, strengths-based. Sample questions:
- "What is your favourite thing you have learned in the manners lessons this year?"
- "Has anything you learned changed how you act at home or with your friends?"
- "Is there anything about the lessons you find boring or difficult to understand?"
- "Do your classmates treat each other more kindly than at the start of the year? Can you give an example?"
- "Is there anything you wish you could learn more about?"

**Do not ask:** Leading questions about specific behavioral changes. Questions about family or home situations. Any question that could cause a child to feel evaluated or compared to peers.

**Coordinator Debrief (15 minutes)**

At the end of the visit, meet with the coordinator privately. Share 2–3 positive observations. Raise 1–2 areas of concern constructively. Ask about upcoming challenges. Confirm next steps.

---

### 7.4 Post-Visit Actions

- [ ] Complete the Monitoring Visit Report (template below, Section 7.6) within 5 working days of the visit.
- [ ] Share a summary of key findings with the school coordinator (email or printed, in Bengali if preferred). This is not a formal report — it is a short "here is what we observed and here is what we are doing" note.
- [ ] If any issues require follow-up (e.g., additional training needed, curriculum materials missing, a safeguarding concern), initiate the relevant response within 10 working days.
- [ ] Update the KPI dashboard with any new data collected during the visit.
- [ ] Flag any systemic issues (patterns across multiple schools) to the Program Manager for escalation.

## 8. Annual External Evaluation

### 8.1 Purpose and Rationale

Internal monitoring, however well-designed, cannot substitute for independent external assessment. External evaluation provides three things internal monitoring cannot: independence (the evaluator has no stake in the findings), methodological rigor (an external researcher applies research-grade standards to data collection and analysis), and credibility (donors and the public trust findings that have not been self-reported). From Year 1, Blu Foundation commits to commissioning an annual external evaluation.

**[FOUNDER INPUT REQUIRED: Budget allocation for external evaluator. Recommended minimum BDT 3,00,000–5,00,000 per year for a qualified Bangladesh-based education researcher. Please confirm: (a) confirmed budget ceiling; (b) whether cost is built into core budget or requires separate donor earmarking; (c) whether Year 1 evaluation scope should be reduced given early program stage.]**

### 8.2 Scope

The external evaluator will conduct an independent assessment of:

1. **Behavioral outcomes:** Pre/post BAT-Student comparison, independently analyzed. The evaluator receives anonymized BAT data collected by Blu Foundation; they also independently administer a sample of BAT instruments across a sub-sample of schools to verify Blu Foundation's data.
2. **Curriculum quality:** Review of the textbook, online course, and lesson plans against recognized frameworks for character education and child development.
3. **Teacher effectiveness:** Observation of at least 10% of program teachers across at least 30% of partner schools. Structured teacher interviews.
4. **Parent and community perception:** Focus groups with parents at a minimum of 3 partner schools.
5. **Organizational effectiveness:** Review of Blu Foundation's M&E systems, data quality, and adherence to this framework.

### 8.3 Evaluation Method

| Method | Detail |
|---|---|
| BAT pre/post comparison | Evaluator-analyzed using Blu Foundation data + independent spot-check sample |
| Classroom observation | Standardized observation checklist; minimum 10% of teachers, 30% of schools |
| Teacher interviews | Semi-structured; minimum 20 teachers |
| Parent focus groups | Minimum 3 focus groups (separate from Blu Foundation staff facilitation) |
| Student focus groups | Minimum 3 focus groups; facilitator independent of Blu Foundation |
| Document review | Curriculum, coordinator reports, monitoring visit reports, financial records |

### 8.4 Evaluator Profile

- Qualification: Masters or PhD in Education, Child Development, Psychology, or related field
- Experience: minimum 5 years in education research or program evaluation
- Bangladesh context: experience working in Bangladesh or South Asia primary education preferred
- Language: Bengali-speaking strongly preferred
- Independence: no current or recent employment or consultancy relationship with Blu Foundation
- Preferred institutional affiliation: BRAC Institute of Educational Development (IED), BRAC University, University of Dhaka (Education or Psychology), Independent University Bangladesh, or equivalent

### 8.5 Evaluation Report

The external evaluator produces a written report in English (with Bengali executive summary) covering:
- Executive summary (maximum 2 pages)
- Methodology
- Findings by domain (behavioral outcomes, curriculum quality, teacher effectiveness, parent perception, organizational effectiveness)
- Recommendations for program improvement
- Appendices: instruments used, data tables, school sample list (anonymized)

**Report to be:**
- Shared with Blu Foundation Executive Director within 6 weeks of fieldwork completion
- Shared (in full, unedited) with major institutional donors within 2 weeks of Blu Foundation receipt
- Published on Blu Foundation website (public version, with school names anonymized unless permission granted)

### 8.6 Year 3 Ambition: Peer-Reviewed Publication

By Year 3, if behavioral outcome data is sufficiently robust, Blu Foundation will explore co-authoring a peer-reviewed journal paper on program outcomes in partnership with the external evaluator and a university partner (BRAC University IED, University of Dhaka, or a UK/US partner institution). This would establish Blu Foundation as a credible contributor to the evidence base on character education in Bangladesh, and would be a significant reputational and fundraising asset. The M&E Officer should begin discussing this possibility with the Year 3 external evaluator from the start of the evaluation process.

## 12. Ethical Standards for Data Collection

These standards are not optional. They apply to all data collected under this framework, by all Blu Foundation staff, school coordinators, and external evaluators.

### 12.1 Informed Consent

All data collection involving children requires **written consent from a parent or guardian** before data is collected. Consent forms must be:
- Written in Bengali (and any other relevant language for the school community) **[NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION]**
- Explained verbally to parents who may need support understanding written text
- Archived securely by Blu Foundation for the duration of data use
- Genuinely voluntary — schools and coordinators must be explicitly instructed that children of non-consenting parents participate fully in the program; only data collection is withheld

Children from Class 3 onward also receive an age-appropriate verbal explanation ("We are going to ask you some questions about how you behave. You do not have to answer if you do not want to. Your answers are private.") before any BAT-Student instrument is administered. This is not a substitute for parental consent; it is an additional assurance of child agency.

### 12.2 Anonymization

Student-level data is anonymized before it is entered into any analysis or reporting system. The process is:

1. Each student is assigned an anonymized code by the school coordinator at the time of BAT administration (e.g., "School 03 / Class 4 / Student 12").
2. The key linking code name to anonymized code is stored at the school only, in a secure location accessible only to the school coordinator and head teacher.
3. Blu Foundation never receives named student data.
4. All reports — internal or external — use aggregated class-level or school-level data. No individual student results appear in any report.

### 12.3 Bengali Language

All parent-facing materials — consent forms, the BAT-Parent survey, parent focus group facilitation guides — must be available in Bengali before the program begins collecting data. **[NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION — all items marked with this flag in this document require professional translation and back-translation check before program launch. A budget line for translation should be included in the Year 1 operational budget.]**

### 12.4 Child-Appropriate Facilitation

Student focus groups (as part of monitoring visits and external evaluation) are facilitated only by:
- Trained Blu Foundation program staff, or
- The external evaluator (or their designated, trained researcher)

No teacher, school management member, or other school staff member may be present during a student focus group. Students must know before the group begins that their answers will not be shared with their teacher. Facilitators use the approved Blu Foundation focus group guide and do not improvise questions that could elicit disclosures of harm or cause distress.

If a child makes a disclosure of harm during a focus group session, the facilitator follows the Blu Foundation Safeguarding Protocol (BLU_D-SG) immediately. Focus group notes are never attributed to individual children.

### 12.5 Data Storage

| Data Type | Storage Location | Access | Retention Period |
|---|---|---|---|
| BAT-Student (paper originals) | Partner school, secure locked storage | School Coordinator and Head Teacher only | Academic year + 1 year, then transferred to Blu Foundation or destroyed |
| BAT-Student (entered data) | Password-protected Blu Foundation server, Bangladesh-based | M&E Officer and Executive Director only | 5 years after program end |
| BAT-Teacher (paper originals) | Partner school, secure locked storage | School Coordinator only | Academic year + 1 year |
| BAT-Parent (paper originals) | Partner school, secure locked storage | School Coordinator only | Academic year + 1 year |
| Monthly coordinator reports | Password-protected Blu Foundation server | M&E Officer, Program Manager, Executive Director | 5 years after program end |
| Monitoring visit reports | Password-protected Blu Foundation server | M&E Officer, Program Manager, Executive Director | 5 years after program end |
| External evaluation data | Evaluator-managed during evaluation; final report archived by Blu Foundation | As above | 5 years after program end |
| Online platform data | Platform provider server; Blu Foundation retains export copies | M&E Officer | 5 years after program end |

### 12.6 Retention and Destruction

All data is retained for **5 years after the conclusion of the program** to which it relates (or 5 years after the end of the student's participation in the program, whichever is later). After the retention period, paper records are physically destroyed (cross-cut shredded). Digital records are permanently deleted with confirmation from the IT system administrator. A destruction log is maintained by the M&E Officer.

### 12.7 Data Breach Protocol

If student data is lost, accessed without authorization, or compromised, the M&E Officer and Executive Director are notified within 24 hours. If the breach involves named student data, affected parents are notified within 72 hours. If the breach constitutes a notifiable event under Bangladesh data protection requirements, Blu Foundation complies with all regulatory notification requirements. Blu Foundation will maintain a data breach log regardless of whether regulatory notification is required.
