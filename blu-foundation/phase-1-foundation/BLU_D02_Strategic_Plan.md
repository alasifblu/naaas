## 1. Executive Summary

Blu Foundation is a Bangladesh-based non-governmental organization with a singular, urgent mission: to rebuild a culture of manners, etiquette, and civic virtue — beginning with primary school children in Classes 1 through 5, and expanding outward to teachers, parents, and the wider community. Bangladesh's primary education system has long deprioritized values education in practice, despite its nominal presence in the national curriculum. Blu Foundation exists to fill that gap with structured, evidence-based, and culturally grounded content, delivered through dedicated textbooks, teacher training, online learning, and community-facing campaigns.

This strategic plan covers three operational years from July 2026 to June 2029. It is organized into three distinct phases that follow an evidence-led logic: pilot, expand, and scale. Each phase builds on verified learning from the phase before it, ensuring that resources are deployed efficiently and that the model is refined before replication.

In Year 1 (July 2026 – June 2027), Blu Foundation will establish its legal registration, operational infrastructure, and board governance. It will partner with two pilot schools in a single urban district, delivering three class-level etiquette textbooks (Classes 1, 2, and 3) to approximately 200 students. Ten teachers will receive intensive residential training in etiquette pedagogy. Rigorous baseline and endline measurement will be conducted, and a Year 1 impact report will be published for donors and partners.

In Year 2 (July 2027 – June 2028), Blu Foundation will expand to 10 schools across three districts and two divisions, deploy all five class-level textbooks, train 50 cumulative teachers, and launch its online learning platform and social media awareness campaign. The organization will submit its first applications to international donors including UNICEF Bangladesh and EU development programs.

In Year 3 (July 2028 – June 2029), Blu Foundation will achieve national profile: 50 schools across five or more divisions, 250 cumulative teachers trained, approximately 10,000 students reached directly, and over 100,000 community members engaged through digital and broadcast campaigns. A formal proposal for curriculum integration will be submitted to the National Curriculum and Textbook Board (NCTB), and a third-party evaluation will validate the three-year impact model.

By 2029, Blu Foundation aims to be recognized as Bangladesh's leading specialist organization in values and etiquette education — a credible partner to government, a compelling grantee to international donors, and a trusted resource to families and communities seeking to restore the social fabric of everyday life.

## 2. Strategic Context

Bangladesh has made remarkable strides in primary education over the past three decades. Gross enrollment rates now exceed 118%, and gender parity at primary level was achieved in 2005. Yet the gains in access have not been matched by gains in quality — and nowhere is that gap more visible than in values, character, and civic education. The national primary curriculum includes a Values (V) subject component on paper, but systematic evidence indicates that this component is routinely omitted, compressed, or treated as a non-examination filler subject in actual classroom delivery [SOURCE_BD_07]. What exists in the curriculum framework is largely aspirational; what happens in classrooms is overwhelmingly driven by examination pressure, rote memorization, and the relentless prioritization of Bengali, Mathematics, and English. Civic education, where it appears, is token-level instruction — its purpose subordinated to the reproduction of factual knowledge rather than the cultivation of moral integrity and prosocial behavior [SOURCE_BD_02]. The result is a generation of children who may be literate but are not schooled in the behavioral dispositions that sustain healthy communities: respect, courtesy, empathy, restraint, and civic responsibility.

The developmental case for intervening at primary level is unambiguous. Early childhood and early primary years represent the optimal window for character formation [SOURCE_BD_03]. Habits of mind, patterns of social behavior, and attitudes toward others are established during this period with a robustness that later interventions struggle to replicate. This is not a novel finding — it is a foundational insight of developmental psychology and education research — but its implications for Bangladesh's curriculum policy have not been operationalized. Non-governmental organizations have been formally identified as key actors in supplementing government curriculum gaps in Bangladesh, particularly in areas where the state lacks either the capacity or the will to deliver quality programming [SOURCE_BD_03]. Critically, NGO pilot programs in Bangladesh have demonstrated systematically higher impacts on both cognitive and social-emotional outcomes than equivalent government programs operating in the same communities [SOURCE_BD_25], suggesting that the flexibility, specialization, and community embeddedness of NGOs constitute genuine structural advantages.

The global evidence base further strengthens the case. Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) programs — the international analogue of what Blu Foundation delivers — produce an average 11-percentile-point improvement in academic achievement alongside measurable improvements in prosocial behavior and reductions in conduct problems [SOURCE_GL_01]. These are not transient gains. Longitudinal research has documented that the positive effects of SEL interventions persist for up to 18 years following the initial program exposure, affecting adult outcomes including employment, relationship quality, and civic participation [SOURCE_GL_02]. Research from the United Kingdom demonstrates that high-quality character education is not confined to elite or faith-based schools — any school type, including under-resourced government institutions, can achieve meaningful and measurable results in nurturing good character when a structured, intentional approach is adopted [SOURCE_UK_01]. These findings are directly applicable to the Bangladesh public primary school context that Blu Foundation targets.

Taken together, the strategic context presents a compelling mandate: the need is real, the evidence for intervention is strong, the NGO delivery model is proven, and the window of developmental opportunity is precisely the age group that Blu Foundation has chosen to serve. The question is not whether this work is necessary — it is how to do it with the discipline, quality, and strategic focus that transforms a good idea into a sustainable institution.

## 3. Strategic Goals (2026–2029)

Blu Foundation's three-year strategy is organized around three mutually reinforcing strategic goals. Each goal is measurable, time-bound, and operationally grounded in the phased work plan described in Sections 4 through 6.

> [FOUNDER INPUT REQUIRED: Review and confirm these three strategic goals align with founder's intent before this document is finalized and shared externally.]

### Goal 1: Curriculum Presence

**Embed structured etiquette education into at least 50 Bangladesh primary schools by 2029.**

Blu Foundation will develop a complete set of five class-level etiquette and manners textbooks (Classes 1–5), translated into Bengali, and deploy these materials into partner schools as a structured supplementary curriculum. Curriculum presence means not merely distributing books, but ensuring that the textbooks are actively used in timetabled class sessions, supported by trained teachers, and monitored through Blu Foundation's M&E framework.

### Goal 2: Teacher Capacity

**Train at least 250 teachers as confident, skilled etiquette educators by 2029.**

Teacher quality is the single strongest predictor of educational outcomes. Blu Foundation will invest disproportionately in teacher training, beginning with a two-day residential workshop model in Year 1 and evolving into a tiered training system — including online professional development modules — by Year 3. Trained teachers will receive ongoing coaching support, structured classroom observation, and access to a community of practice.

### Goal 3: Community Awareness

**Reach 100,000 or more community members through digital and social campaigns by 2029.**

Etiquette and civic virtue cannot be taught in schools alone. The behavioral norms children learn must be reinforced at home, in neighborhoods, and in public life. Blu Foundation will build a sustained public communication presence through social media campaigns, parent workshops, online courses, and — in Year 3 — a national television and radio campaign. The community awareness goal positions Blu Foundation as a voice in the national conversation about character and civic culture, not merely a curriculum provider.

## 4. Year 1 — Pilot Phase (July 2026 – June 2027)

### Context

Year 1 is the foundation upon which everything else is built. Its purpose is twofold: to establish the legal, operational, and governance infrastructure that allows Blu Foundation to function as a credible NGO; and to deliver a rigorous, well-documented pilot that generates the evidence base needed to attract donors, expand partnerships, and refine the model before replication. Every decision in Year 1 — the selection of pilot schools, the design of the textbooks, the structure of teacher training, the M&E methodology — must be made with Year 2 scalability in mind. A pilot that is poorly designed or inadequately documented is not a foundation; it is a liability.

### Key Activities by Quarter

**Q1 — July to September 2026: Legal, Governance, and Content Foundations**

The first quarter is infrastructure-intensive. Blu Foundation will complete NGO registration with the Department of Social Services (DSS) and obtain clearance from the NGO Affairs Bureau (NGOAB). The founding board will be constituted, with a minimum of five members covering education, legal, finance, and community representation. A project office will be established. Simultaneously, the content team will complete the design and Bengali translation of the three pilot textbooks (BLU_D05, D06, D07), with peer review by at least two qualified educators and one Islamic scholar to ensure cultural appropriateness. [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION] Formal partnership agreements with the two pilot schools will be negotiated and signed, including headteacher endorsement, dedicated class time, and M&E cooperation clauses.

**Q2 — October to December 2026: Launch, Training, and Baseline**

With textbooks finalized and printed (600 copies total — 200 per class level), Q2 marks the operational launch of the pilot. A two-day residential teacher training workshop will be delivered to the 10 pilot teachers, covering: the evidence base for etiquette education; pedagogical approaches for Class 1–3; how to use each textbook chapter; classroom management during etiquette lessons; and how to complete the M&E behavioral assessment forms. Baseline behavioral assessments will be administered to all enrolled students using the BLU_D17 assessment tool. The Class 1–3 curriculum will launch in both pilot schools in October 2026, with weekly timetabled etiquette lessons.

**Q3 — January to March 2027: Mid-Year Review and Community Engagement**

By Q3, teachers will have been delivering etiquette lessons for approximately three months. Blu Foundation staff will conduct structured classroom observations in all four class groups (two schools × two classes minimum), using the fidelity observation checklist from BLU_D17. Findings from observations will inform a structured feedback session with each school's teaching team. Two parent workshops will be held at each school (four total), introducing parents to the etiquette curriculum, modeling key behaviors, and inviting them to reinforce lessons at home. Blu Foundation's social media presence — Facebook and YouTube, optimized for Bangladeshi audiences — will soft launch, with weekly content drawing on textbook themes.

**Q4 — April to June 2027: Endline Assessment, Impact Report, and Year 2 Planning**

Endline behavioral assessments will be administered to all enrolled students. Pre-post data will be analyzed against the baseline using the BLU_D17 tool, measuring behavioral improvement across five competency domains: respectful communication, punctuality, personal hygiene and presentation, social courtesy, and civic awareness. A Year 1 Impact Report will be prepared in both English and Bengali [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION], presenting evidence of reach, process quality, and behavioral outcomes. The report will be shared with pilot school headteachers, NGOAB, and prospective donors at a formal donor briefing event. Year 2 planning — including school selection, textbook production for Classes 4 and 5, and expansion budget — will be finalized.

## 5. Year 2 — Expansion Phase (July 2027 – June 2028)

### Context

Year 2 is the proof-of-scalability phase. Building on the documented evidence and refined model from the pilot, Blu Foundation will scale its school reach fivefold, deploy the complete five-class textbook curriculum, train five times as many teachers, and launch its digital presence — including an online learning platform for parents and community members. Year 2 also marks the beginning of Blu Foundation's transition from a purely grant-funded startup toward a more diversified funding base, with the first applications to major international donors and the launch of fee-generating online courses.

### Year 2 Targets

| Parameter | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Schools enrolled (cumulative) | 10 (spanning 3 districts, 2 divisions) |
| Classes covered | Class 1 through Class 5 (all levels) |
| Textbooks in active use | All 5 class-level textbooks (BLU_D05 through BLU_D09) |
| Students reached (cumulative) | ~1,000 |
| Teachers trained (cumulative) | 50 |
| Online learning platform | Launched and active |
| Social media campaign | Active, with 12-week structured campaign |

### Key Activities by Quarter

**Q1 — July to September 2027: Expansion, Content, and New Cohort Training**

Eight new partner schools will be recruited across two additional districts, with geographic diversity (urban and peri-urban) intentionally built into the selection criteria. Bengali translations of the full Class 4 and Class 5 textbooks (BLU_D08 and BLU_D09) will be completed and peer-reviewed. A new cohort of 40 teachers will be trained through the residential workshop model, now refined based on Year 1 feedback. The Year 1 cohort of 10 teachers will be offered a one-day refresher and upgraded to the expanded Class 4–5 content.

**Q2 — October to December 2027: Digital Launch and Awareness Campaign**

The Blu Foundation online learning platform will launch, hosting Course 3 from BLU_D11 — an adapted parent-focused version of the etiquette curriculum — with video modules, downloadable resources, and completion certificates. The Blu Foundation website will go live in both English and Bengali [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION], serving as the organization's public face for donors, partners, media, and families. A structured 12-week social media campaign will begin, with three posts per week across Facebook, YouTube, and — where audience analytics support it — TikTok Bangladesh. All content will be produced in Bengali with English captions for international audiences.

**Q3 — January to March 2028: M&E, Donor Engagement, and Applications**

Mid-year classroom observations will be conducted across all 10 partner schools. The first formal donor impact report — covering cumulative reach, behavioral outcomes, teacher feedback, and financial accountability — will be produced and shared with current funders and prospective partners. Blu Foundation will submit applications to at least three international donor programs: the UNICEF Innovation Fund, the European Union's CSO Development program for Bangladesh, and the Australian Aid NGO Cooperation Program (ANCP). Grant-writing support will be engaged if internal capacity is insufficient.

**Q4 — April to June 2028: Endline, 3-District Report, and Year 3 Planning**

Endline behavioral assessments will be conducted across all 10 schools. A 3-District Impact Report will be published, providing disaggregated data by district, school type, class level, and gender. Year 3 planning will begin immediately: school pipeline identification for expansion to 50 schools, regional coordinator recruitment, NCTB engagement strategy, and Year 3 budget finalization.

## 6. Year 3 — Scale Phase (July 2028 – June 2029)

### Context

Year 3 transforms Blu Foundation from a credible regional NGO into a nationally recognized institution. The scale phase is characterized by three strategic priorities that operate simultaneously: expanding school reach to 50 schools across five or more divisions; engaging government formally through the Ministry of Primary and Mass Education (MoPME) and NCTB for curriculum integration; and launching the national community awareness campaign that will carry the Blu Foundation brand — and its message about the value of manners, etiquette, and civic virtue — to 100,000 community members. Year 3 also commissions the independent third-party impact evaluation that will provide the rigorous, externally validated evidence base needed for long-term donor confidence and government partnership.

### Year 3 Targets

| Parameter | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Schools enrolled (cumulative) | 50 (national presence, 5+ divisions, urban and semi-urban) |
| Students reached (cumulative) | ~10,000 |
| Teachers trained (cumulative) | 250 |
| Community members reached via campaigns | 100,000+ |
| Government engagement | Formal proposal submitted to NCTB for curriculum integration |
| National awareness campaign | 1 national TV and radio campaign |
| Third-party evaluation | Commissioned and completed |

### Key Activities by Quarter

**Q1 — July to September 2028: Scale Partnerships, New Courses, and Regional Staffing**

School partnership agreements will be signed for the expansion from 10 to 50 schools, with new schools distributed across at least five divisions including Dhaka, Chattogram, Rajshahi, Khulna, and Sylhet or Barishal. Regional field coordinators — one per division — will be recruited and inducted. The community leader online course (Course 5 from BLU_D11) will launch, targeting ward-level elected representatives, mosque and temple committees, community health workers, and neighborhood association leaders. A third cohort of teachers will be trained, bringing the cumulative total toward the 250 target.

**Q2 — October to December 2028: Government Engagement and International Donors**

A formal partnership proposal will be submitted to NCTB, recommending the integration of Blu Foundation's etiquette curriculum modules as optional supplementary material within the national primary curriculum framework. This proposal will be accompanied by three years of behavioral impact data, an independent technical review, and endorsement letters from headteachers, parents, and community leaders. Simultaneously, Blu Foundation's leadership team will conduct a structured international donor engagement tour — meeting with UNICEF Bangladesh, the EU Delegation, and USAID Bangladesh — presenting the Year 1 and Year 2 evidence base and inviting multi-year funding commitments. A third-party impact evaluation firm will be commissioned through a competitive tender, with the evaluation framework designed to assess behavioral outcomes, curriculum fidelity, teacher confidence, and community attitude change.

**Q3 — January to March 2029: National Campaign Launch and Media Strategy**

The national television and radio awareness campaign — the first of its kind for etiquette education in Bangladesh — will air across at least two national television channels and three national FM radio stations over a six-week period. Campaign content will be developed with a professional media agency and will draw directly on Blu Foundation's textbook themes, translated into emotionally resonant short-form storytelling. A targeted press coverage strategy will seek editorial placements in The Daily Star, Prothom Alo [NEEDS BENGALI TRANSLATION], and the Daily Bangladesh. The third-party evaluation will conduct its field data collection during this quarter, covering a stratified sample of schools across all five divisions.

**Q4 — April to June 2029: 3-Year Impact Report, Sustainability Planning, and Charter of Renewal**

The three-year impact report — Blu Foundation's flagship evidence document — will be published and launched at a national event attended by government representatives, donor partners, school headteachers, media, and community leaders. The third-party evaluation report will be appended to or published alongside this report. The board will adopt a formal organizational sustainability plan, mapping the transition from grant dependency toward earned revenue and government partnership. A Charter of Renewal — a public commitment to the next three-year period (2029–2032) — will be adopted by the board and shared publicly.

## 9. Partnerships Strategy

### Government Partnerships: Building the Pathway to Policy Change

Blu Foundation's long-term ambition — the integration of structured etiquette education into Bangladesh's national primary curriculum — can only be achieved through a sustained, evidence-led engagement with government. The strategic approach to government partnership is deliberately patient and non-confrontational. In Years 1 and 2, Blu Foundation will invest in building informal relationships with staff-level officials at MoPME and NCTB — sharing evidence, inviting school visits, and demonstrating that the organization's methodology is rigorous, culturally appropriate, and complementary to the national curriculum rather than competitive with it. The formal NCTB proposal in Year 3 will not be a cold submission; it will be the natural next step in a two-year relationship built on shared interest in child development outcomes. Blu Foundation will also engage the Directorate of Primary Education (DPE) and the Primary Teachers Training Institutes (PTIs) as natural institutional allies for the teacher training component, exploring opportunities to integrate Blu Foundation's etiquette training modules into the PTI professional development calendar from Year 2 onward.

### NGO, Academic, and Civil Society Partnerships

The NGO ecosystem in Bangladesh offers Blu Foundation both models to learn from and potential delivery partnerships to explore. BRAC Education Programme — the world's largest NGO education system — has deep reach into under-served communities that Blu Foundation cannot replicate independently. Rather than competing, Blu Foundation should explore co-delivery arrangements in Year 3, where BRAC's community infrastructure is paired with Blu Foundation's specialist etiquette curriculum. CARE Bangladesh and Save the Children operate significant child development programs where etiquette and social-emotional content could complement existing programming. Academic institutions — particularly BRAC University's Institute of Educational Development (IED) and the University of Dhaka Faculty of Education — can serve as independent peer reviewers of Blu Foundation's textbooks and evaluation tools, lending academic credibility to the organization's evidence claims. Research partnerships with these institutions, formalized in Years 2 and 3, will strengthen Blu Foundation's standing with international donors who expect academic rigor. The evidence is clear that dedicated staffing for partnership development produces the best outcomes [SOURCE_UK_02]; Blu Foundation will designate a named partnerships lead from Year 2 onward rather than distributing this responsibility informally across program staff.

### Corporate and International Funding Partnerships

Corporate social responsibility funding in Bangladesh is growing, particularly among telecommunications, banking, and consumer goods companies that face regulatory CSR requirements. Blu Foundation offers corporate partners a compelling, visible, and socially resonant investment: named textbooks, teacher cohort sponsorship, and direct access to community goodwill in underserved areas. The CSR partnership model will be developed in Year 2 with a dedicated proposal template and tiered sponsorship structure. On the international funding side, Blu Foundation will align its grant applications with the strategic priorities of UNICEF Bangladesh (child development and education quality), UNESCO (values education and cultural heritage), the European Union (civil society strengthening), and Australian Aid (governance, education, and gender equity). Applications will be timed for Q3 of Year 2, when the organization has a full year of pilot evidence to present. Multi-year grant commitments from at least one international donor will be a Year 3 organizational objective, providing the financial stability necessary to sustain operations beyond the founder's initial seed investment.

## 12. Organizational Sustainability

### Funding Architecture: Grants to Diversification

In Years 1 and 2, Blu Foundation will be primarily grant-funded — this is appropriate and expected for an early-stage NGO establishing credibility and building its evidence base. Founder seed capital will cover the initial months of Year 1 operations, bridging the gap before the first donor grants are received. Small grants from embassy development funds, local philanthropic foundations, and corporate CSR partnerships will provide the Year 1 budget. Year 2 will see the first applications to larger international donors (UNICEF, EU, Australian Aid), with multi-year grant decisions expected in Year 3. Alongside this grant funding, Year 2 will introduce Blu Foundation's first earned revenue stream: the parent online course (Course 3 from BLU_D11) will be offered on a fee-for-access basis to families outside partner schools, with a subsidized or free tier for low-income users. Textbook licensing fees — charged to private schools and international organizations that wish to use the Blu Foundation curriculum without direct program support — represent an additional earned revenue pathway from Year 3. By the end of Year 3, the organization targets a minimum of 20% of its operating budget from earned revenue, with the balance from grants and government partnership contributions.

### Long-Term Sustainability Vision

From Year 3 onward, Blu Foundation's sustainability model will rest on three pillars: government partnership (if NCTB integration is achieved, government per-pupil or per-teacher funding becomes available), diversified international grants, and earned income from digital courses and licensing. The organization will avoid dependency on any single funder, capping any individual donor's share of total revenue at 40%. A board-managed operational reserve, equivalent to six months of operating costs, will be established by the end of Year 3. The organizational sustainability plan, commissioned in Q4 Year 3, will model the full 2029–2032 financial scenario under optimistic, base-case, and conservative assumptions.

## 13. Alignment with SDGs

Blu Foundation's work is directly aligned with three of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. This alignment is not rhetorical; it is substantive, measurable, and directly embedded in the organization's theory of change and impact framework.

| SDG | Goal Title | Blu Foundation Contribution |
|-----|------------|------------------------------|
| SDG 4 | Quality Education | Blu Foundation develops and delivers a structured, evidence-based etiquette and values curriculum for primary school children in Classes 1–5. Its teacher training program directly improves classroom pedagogical quality in government schools that are otherwise under-resourced for character education. By 2029, 250 trained teachers in 50 schools will be delivering measurably improved educational experiences. The organization's online learning platform extends educational access beyond school walls to parents and community members, contributing to lifelong learning. |
| SDG 10 | Reduced Inequalities | Blu Foundation's textbooks and training programs are delivered free of charge to government primary schools serving low- and lower-middle-income communities. Elite private schools in Bangladesh routinely offer character, etiquette, and co-curricular programs that government schools cannot afford. Blu Foundation narrows this inequality by bringing structured, high-quality values education — previously accessible only to wealthier families — into under-resourced public schools. The online course platform uses a subsidized access model to ensure that low-income parents are not excluded from the community learning content. |
| SDG 16 | Peace, Justice, and Strong Institutions | The behavioral competencies that Blu Foundation teaches — respectful communication, civic awareness, courtesy, empathy, and personal integrity — are precisely the dispositions that underpin peaceful communities and trustworthy institutions. Children who internalize these values from primary school age are more likely to grow into adults who participate constructively in civic life, resolve conflicts non-violently, and hold institutions to account with dignity. By building civic virtue from the ground up, Blu Foundation contributes to the long-term social infrastructure of a more just and stable Bangladesh. |
